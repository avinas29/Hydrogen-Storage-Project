8.2The proposed experimental programme, in plain words Report §8.3

Nothing here was done. It is a plan. Nine alloys from the screening: LaNi₅ and TiFe as benchmarks (known behaviour, to check the equipment); Ti₁.₂CrMn, Ti₀.₉Zr₀.₂Mn₁.₄Cr₀.₄V₀.₂ and Ti₀.₅₆V₀.₃Zr₀.₁Mn₀.₉₄Fe₀.₀₆Ni₀.₀₄ from the mobile tier (the last because its data conflict); TiFe₀.₉Al₀.₁, Ti₀.₉Zr₀.₁Mn₁.₄, TiFe₀.₈₅Mn₀.₁₅ and CaNi₅ from the stationary tier. Plus two blind tests of the model: LaNi₄.₂Al₀.₈ (predicted 38.7, interval 34.3–41.6) and TiFe₀.₇₅Mn₀.₂₅ (32.0, 25.7–37.8). The model "passes" if the measured ΔH falls in the interval.

8.3Future modelling and platform work Report §8.4

  • Add structural information (C14/C15, lattice parameters).
  • Model ΔS, or the whole Van't Hoff line, instead of assuming it.
  • Active learning: measure the candidates the model is least sure about, add them, retrain, and the domain grows where experiments are made.
  • A system-level model (vessel, heat exchanger, porosity) for system capacity and refuelling time.
  • Quantitative cost and kinetic data where available.
Defend
Q8.1What is the weakest part of your project?

The absence of an independent test: the two datasets share most values, and no new alloys were measured. The cross-validation is done carefully (grouped, nested), but a blind test on new alloys would be the real proof. That is why two blind-test compositions with stated intervals are proposed.

Q8.2If you had six more months, what would you do first?

Synthesise and PCT-test the two blind-test alloys and the benchmarks, to check the model against new measurements. Then add XRD phase information as a descriptor, and build a ΔS model so the temperature bands shrink.

Q8.3Why choose LaNi₅ and TiFe for the experiments when you already know their values?

They are benchmarks. If my set-up reproduces their known plateaus, I can trust its measurements on the new alloys.

Q8.4What is a Sieverts apparatus?

A volumetric set-up: a calibrated reservoir of hydrogen at known pressure and temperature is opened to the sample; the pressure drop tells how much hydrogen was absorbed. Repeating step by step traces the PCT curve.
